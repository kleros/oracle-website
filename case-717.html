Overview

A question no data feed can answer

Seer keeps a list of verified markets, and anyone can challenge a market on it. The challenger argued this one broke Seer’s policy: “Jesus” and “return” mean different things to different people, so nobody could say for sure how it resolves. The submitter argued the policy already covers that: the market resolves on credible reporting, and jurors don’t judge theology.

This is the kind of question where a jury matters. The jurors didn’t decide whether Jesus returns. They decided whether the market follows the written policy. Most markets never reach a court, and most disputes end in one round. This one used every step, so it shows how the whole process works.

Challenger

Challenged the listing and opened the case. Argued the market is ambiguous (Article 4) and its answer is already known (Article 9).

Submitter

Submitted the market and appealed after each loss. Argued it resolves on credible reporting (Article 18) about the most obvious meaning (Article 12).

Arguments

What each side argued

Don’t list it

  • Article 4, clarity. The policy rejects markets “subject to different interpretations”. Christian, Islamic, Jewish, Hindu and historical readings of “Jesus” and a “return” differ.
  • No way to verify. If someone claims to be Jesus, who decides? There is no test a court can apply.
  • Article 9, answer already known. Leaving belief aside, the historical figure died around AD 30–33.
  • The right document. The stricter Verified Markets policy applies, not only the general resolution policy.

List it

  • Article 18, credible reporting. If credible sources report a return it resolves Yes, otherwise No. Jurors don’t judge theology.
  • Article 12, most obvious entity. Fringe readings don’t create ambiguity, or markets on “recession” or “war” would fail too.
  • Article 9 bars past events, not future events someone thinks impossible.
  • Precedent. Similar markets were accepted before.

Jurors

Paid to vote the way the ruling goes

Each seat puts PNK at stake. Jurors who vote with the final ruling keep their stake, earn the fee, and share the stake lost by the others. Jurors who vote against it, or don’t vote, lose it. The chance of being drawn grows with the PNK staked, so a juror can hold several seats.

Costs

Every round costs more

To appeal, both sides fund the next round, and anyone can contribute. The side that lost the last round pays 3× the new jury fee, the side that won pays 2×. The jurors’ fee comes out of that, and the rest goes to whoever funded the side that wins in the end.

All amounts in xDAI, the stable token used for fees on Gnosis Chain. Across the case, jurors were paid 552 xDAI in fees, and 16,704 PNK moved from jurors who voted against the ruling to jurors who voted with it.
